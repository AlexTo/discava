A student can rate a course from 1 to 5 stars and add a written review. Ratings show on the course page, on course cards and in search.

## For students

Open the course page and find the **Reviews** section. If you are enrolled, you can:

- **Post** a review: pick a star rating (required) and optionally write up to 2,000 characters.
- **Edit** your review. It shows an "edited" mark, and it still counts once.
- **Delete** your review, after confirming. The course goes back to the summary it had without it, and you can post a new one.

Each student has one review per course. Posting twice at once, for example by double clicking, makes one review. You need to be enrolled, and a student who dropped the course can't post a new review. You can still edit or delete a review you already wrote, whatever the course's status. A course that is not published takes no new reviews.

Visitors who are signed out see the summary and the reviews, with a prompt to sign in and enrol. Signed-in students who are not enrolled are asked to enrol.

## How ratings appear

The course page shows the average to one decimal (for example 4.6), the number of ratings and a breakdown of how many gave each star count. Cards in the catalogue and in search show the same average and count, or "No ratings yet".

## How search uses ratings

Search can sort by **Relevance**, **Highest rated** or **Newest**, and filter by a minimum rating (4.5, 4.0, 3.5 or 3.0 and up). The minimum filter uses the plain average, and courses with no ratings never match it.

Ordering by rating and the small lift ratings give in relevance don't use the plain average, so a single 5-star rating can't beat a course with many good ones. Search treats every course as if it had five extra ratings of 3.5 stars:

> weighted rating = (number of ratings × average + 5 × 3.5) ÷ (number of ratings + 5)

One 5-star rating gives 3.75. Twenty ratings averaging 4.5 give 4.3, so that course ranks higher. Under **Highest rated**, unrated courses come last. Under **Relevance**, the text match still dominates: the weighted rating only nudges the score by a few percent, and unrated courses get no change. A rating change reaches search within seconds.

## Instructor responses

On a course's page in the instructor portal, **Reviews** shows the average, the breakdown and each review. An instructor of the course can **Respond** to a review (up to 1,000 characters), edit the response or delete it. Any instructor of the course can change another's response. The response is shown under the review on the course page, with the instructor's name, and doesn't change the rating or mark the review edited. A removed review takes no response.

## Admin removal

An admin can remove a review that breaks the rules, from **Reviews** in the admin portal: pick the course, then **Remove** and give a reason (up to 200 characters). A removed review leaves the course page, cards, the average and search. Instructors of the course can't remove reviews.

The author sees "Your review was removed by a moderator" with the reason, and no form: they can't post another review for that course.

## Privacy

Reviews show the author's display name in the short form, such as "Alex T.", never their email. Instructors get the student's ID only to answer the review.

## Limitations

- No "helpful" votes.
- No way to report a review: removal is by an admin.
- No minimum progress is needed: any enrolled student can review straight away.
